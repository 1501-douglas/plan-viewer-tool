What the 3D model is for

The 3D model is the same owner design as the plan viewer, built up so you can walk through it. It opens with a short welcome and an optional guided tour. In it you can:

  • Walk each level at eye height, or look down on it as a dollhouse
  • Turn on the wiring, ducts, plumbing and fire-separation layers
  • Compare finish concepts and try furniture arrangements (kept in your own browser)
  • See what each object rests on, under Show assumptions
  • Jump between a room on the plan and the same room in 3D

It is not an approved construction document and not to dimension: walls are traced from the drawings to within about 4 in, and nothing is field-measured yet. Finishes are concepts, not selections. A link to a particular view is a convenience; it does not make that view approved. It needs a recent browser; a phone works.

What the viewer is for

The viewer shows the owner's design intent on one plan: layout, lighting and power, heating and cooling, and plumbing fixtures. It also lists the questions that are still open. In it you can:

  • Switch between the first floor and the lower level
  • Turn layers on and off, or pick one trade at a time
  • Zoom to a room
  • See the open items, split into resolve before submission, confirm later and coordination points
  • See what is demolished, what is new and what moves, on the Existing / demo / new layer
  • Look at the twelve owner sheets: layouts, lighting and power, existing / demo / new (SC-1 to SC-3), and Owner E, Owner M and Owner P

It is not the permit set, an engineered design, or a material or finish schedule. It does not certify that anything is coordinated. It shows the owner's intent as of the date printed in it.
